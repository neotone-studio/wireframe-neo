Privacy

What is collected, why, for how long, and how to get it back or have it deleted. It applies to every reader, including one who never orders anything, which is why it comes first.

Reviewed against the current policy at digitalhandpan.com/privacy-policy.pdf. That document is a Japanese manufacturer's template with the names changed: it cites the Personal Information Protection Law and the Personal Information Protection Committee, which are Japan's statute and regulator, and refers to group companies, a distributor, repaired products being dispatched, and servers outside "our plant." A GDPR section is appended, so obligations are stated twice under two regimes, one of which does not apply.

Cut
  • The entire Japanese half. Sections resting on the Personal Information Protection Law are not the law you operate under. GDPR and the Hungarian implementation are.
  • Group companies, distributors, subsidiaries, the plant. There is one company and one workshop.
  • Behavioural advertising via Google. The policy discloses targeted advertising cookies. If that is not running, it declares processing that does not happen. If it is, a footer opt-out link is not consent.
  • "2022 DigitalOcean, LLC" as the named host. That is a copyright string pasted in as a party.
  • Survey, contest and event language. None of it exists.
Missing
  • The real categories of data. The policy declares name and email. You take orders: postal address, telephone, payment handled by a processor, and a serial number tied to a person for the life of the instrument.
  • neOS accounts and the Scale Gallery. An account, a public display name, and user-created scales published to other players. None of it is mentioned, and it is the most personal thing the company holds.
  • Named processors. Payment provider, email sender, host, and anything the software talks to. A list, kept current.
  • Retention periods that are actual periods. "As long as necessary" is not one. An instrument warranty gives a real number to anchor to.
  • Controller identity in full. Registered company name, seat, company registration number, and a contact route that is not only an email address.
  • Cookies, honestly. Easiest version: run none beyond what the site needs to function, say so in a sentence, and never build a consent banner. A site whose principle is answering questions where they arise should not open with a modal asking permission to track.

Terms of sale

The document that does not currently exist, and the one with real consequences. Someone is paying five figures for an object that will not exist for another sixty days.

The One page already states purchase terms inline, where the question arises. This section is what those statements have to be consistent with. If the two ever disagree, the customer relies on the one they read at the moment of ordering, which is the inline one.

Cover
  • Right of withdrawal. Fourteen days on a distance sale, when the clock starts, and how to exercise it. Whether a made-to-order instrument in a chosen wood is exempt as a personalised good is exactly the question to get answered rather than assumed.
  • The two year guarantee of conformity , separate from and additional to any warranty offered voluntarily. Say which is which, because buyers conflate them.
  • Lead time as a commitment. "~60 days" appears in the specs. What happens at day 90, and what the buyer can do about it.
  • Deposit, balance and cancellation. When money is taken, what is refundable once wood has been cut, and what is not.
  • B-stock. What the grade means, what is excluded, and whether the guarantee differs.
  • Delivery, risk and customs. When risk passes, who clears the instrument, and who pays duty outside the EU.
  • Repairs. An instrument that comes back to Budapest and returns: shipping, timescale, what is covered.
  • Referral discounts. Who is eligible, whether codes stack, and how long one is good for. The wireframe already enforces rules that no document states.
  • The software is part of the product. A buyer purchases an instrument whose behaviour changes with updates. Say what is guaranteed to keep working, and for how long.

Terms of website use

The shortest of the three, and the one currently doing the least. Reading this site creates almost no relationship: there is no account, nothing to post, and nothing to agree to.

Reviewed against digitalhandpan.com/terms-of-use.pdf. US-style boilerplate: all-caps disclaimers, attorneys' fees, and "the laws of the EU" in the governing law clause. The EU is not a jurisdiction that can be chosen. It also governs message boards and user registration, neither of which exists, and spells the company "Sounventure" once.

Cut
  • The Submissions clause, which is the one actively working against the company. It states that anything a user posts becomes Soundventure's property, free to exploit commercially. Read against the Scale Gallery, that claims ownership of scales players made. It should be a licence: the player keeps the scale, Neotone gets the right to host, display and distribute it, and can say so in one sentence.
  • Message boards and registration requirements. Neither exists on this site.
  • Blanket exclusion of all liability and all warranties. Largely unenforceable against consumers here, so it buys little and reads as hostile from a company whose whole argument is that it is serious.
  • The prohibition on saving or printing pages. Unenforceable, and strange on a site built to be read.
Fix or add
  • Governing law and venue: Hungary, and a named court, with the note that consumers keep the protections of their own country.
  • Who publishes this site, in a line: registered name, seat, registration number, contact.
  • Editorial content is the company's, and quotation with attribution is welcome. Cheaper to say than to litigate, and it matches how the writing is meant to travel.
  • Scale Gallery rules, if they live anywhere: what may be shared, what happens when a scale is unshared, and the fact that a copy already added to someone's list stays with them. The software already behaves this way; nothing states it.
  • The company name spelled correctly.
