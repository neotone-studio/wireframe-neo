Why the note names change as you build

The Scale Builder respells notes as you place them. What looks like a quirk is the instrument reading what kind of scale you are making.

Build a D Kurd and something quiet happens. You place the note your ear calls an A sharp, and the instrument writes it down as a B flat.

Nothing has changed about the pitch. The frequency is identical, the tonefield is identical, what you hear is identical. What changed is the name, and the name is the instrument telling you what it thinks you are building.

A scale is a set of positions

A scale is not a list of pitches. It is a list of positions relative to a root, and every position has a conventional name. The second degree of a Kurd is a flattened second, so it is written as a flat. Writing it as a raised first would describe the same sound and the wrong idea.

This is why the sharps and flats toggle governs only the root. Once the instrument knows the root and recognises the shape you are assembling, every other spelling follows from it. Choose D as your root and the flattened second is a B flat. Choose C sharp and the same position becomes a D natural, because a C sharp Kurd is spelled from a different starting point.

Why the instrument does this at all

It would be simpler to store pitches and print whatever name was typed. Most software does. The cost lands later, when a player writes a scale down to share it, or reads one from a maker, and the notation says something different from what the scale is.

The Scale Builder is doing the thing a musician does without thinking: hearing a position rather than a pitch, and writing it the way it functions. Getting that right is a small piece of lutherie, done in software rather than in metal.
