Scales

Written in three registers. Read the first to get going, the second when you want the rest of it, the third when you want to know why the instrument behaves the way it does.

Basics

Enough to build a scale and load it.

Before you start: firmware Check you are on 7.2 or higher.

Open Instrument in the main menu and check the firmware version. If it is below 7.2, connect the instrument to WiFi, power it off, and power it back on. The update runs on its own.

The Scale Gallery The public collection, from players and from acoustic makers.

The Gallery holds every scale available on the instrument, listed alphabetically and searchable. Some were shared by Neotone players, others come from acoustic handpan makers. It grows without you doing anything.

Anatomy of a scale card What a card tells you, and the two things you can do with it.

Each card carries the name, the maker who added it, and the notes with the root underlined. From there you can load it onto the instrument straight away, or add it to My Scales.

Diagram: the parts of a scale card.

My Scales Your own list, and what ships on a new instrument.

My Scales is the list you curate: the ones you reach for, and the ones you have built yourself and may not have shared. A new instrument arrives with a handful already loaded, including common handpan scales and a few mutants that sit at the edge of what can be built acoustically.

Quick Load Map scales to tonefields so you can load them without the software.

Quick Load assigns any scale from My Scales to a tonefield. On the instrument, hold the ding and that tonefield together to preview the scale name, and hold a moment longer to load it. This is how you change scales away from the software, and how most players set up a live set.

Diagram: standard and mutant tonefield map.

The Scale Builder Building from scratch, or from something close.

The Builder gives you a blank layout: choose notes, place them where you want them, name the scale, and save. If an existing scale is nearly right, duplicating it is usually faster than starting over.

Going deeper

The rest of it, once you have your bearings.

The root, sharps and flats Why the toggle applies only to the root.

The root sits on the ding by default, and you can move it to any tonefield. The sharps and flats toggle governs how the root is spelled, and every other note follows from that choice.

Editing, duplicating, deleting What happens to copies when you change your mind.

Editing reopens the scale in the Builder with everything as you left it. Duplicating gives you a copy you own, sitting above the original. Deleting asks first, and only removes your own copy.

Sharing to the gallery What unsharing does, and what it leaves alone.

Toggling Public Gallery on publishes the scale under your name. Toggling it off stops anyone new adding it, and leaves existing copies where they are. The same rule protects you: a scale you have added stays yours.

Scale matching Finding scales that share notes with one you have.

Show More on any card opens the full layout, the unique notes, and the matching tools for finding neighbouring scales.
